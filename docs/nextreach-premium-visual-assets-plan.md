## 1. Creative thesis

NextReach Studio should feel like a warm architectural engineering studio that shows real digital work moving from idea to system.

The signature is:

- Warm frosted glass over charcoal and cream architectural surfaces.
- Terracotta as the only signal color.
- One recurring signal line representing “reach what’s next”.
- Real interfaces and recordings as proof.
- One cinematic hero moment followed by calm editorial sections.
- Motion that explains hierarchy, mechanism, or ownership.

Working name: **Reach Signal / Warm Architectural Glass**.

### Homepage story: Reach → Build → Own

- **Reach:** the hero explains what NextReach builds; selected real demos establish visual credibility immediately.
- **Build:** a small asymmetric service showcase and real interaction recordings explain websites, products, apps, and automation.
- **Own:** process and ownership show the handover, then a simple project CTA closes the page.

Keep pricing, factual claims, research links, tools, and navigation intact. Reduce duplicate prose rather than hiding useful information inside artwork. A dark media frame can contrast with the cream canvas; do not randomly invert whole sections.

Do not make the site look like a generic AI agency, neon SaaS template, or collection of unrelated glassmorphism experiments.

These are existing website palette variants, not a replacement brand palette. `BRANDING.md` also defines charcoal `#2A2A2D`, canvas `#F5F1E9`, and stone `#A69D92`; use those for original identity collateral when required. Preserve the same terracotta and font families. Check actual text contrast: stone gray is not suitable for small text on a cream background without a darker variant. Prompts mentioning hex colors are approximate; match critical colors in the finishing color grade.

### Fonts

- Display: Cabinet Grotesk / local `Studio Cabinet`
- Body: Plus Jakarta Sans / local `Studio Jakarta`
- Technical labels: JetBrains Mono / local `Studio Mono`

Typography direction: hero weight 550–650, section heading weight 500–600, body weight 400–500, sparse mono labels, and minimal all-caps text.

### Glass recipe

Reserve glass for navigation, hero controls, hero project cards, media frames, small utility panels, and ownership layers.

- Light surface: `rgba(255,255,255,0.56–0.78)`.
- Dark surface: `rgba(23,26,24,0.58–0.82)`.
- Blur: 8–18px desktop; disabled or replaced on mobile.
- Saturation: 115–140% maximum.
- Border: 1px white or charcoal edge with 10–25% opacity.
- Highlight: one top inset line.
- Shadow: broad, low-opacity, charcoal or terracotta tinted.
- No outer neon glow.

## 5. Hero ambient loop

### Technical brief

- Duration: 5 seconds.
- Perfect loop with matching first and final frames.
- 24 or 30fps.
- Desktop master: 1920×1080.
- Mobile crop: 1080×1350 or 1080×1920.
- No audio, text, logos, UI, people, hands, or recognizable stock objects.
- Slow motion with no flashing, fast caustics, or high-frequency noise.
- Subject stays within the central 70% safe area.


### Loop QA and fallback

- Use the same still as first and last frame if the video tool supports endpoint references.
- Review at least three repeats, then inspect the seam frame-by-frame. Check silhouette, background luminance, line position, shadow, and reflection velocity; matching appearance is not enough if movement snaps.
- A 5-second loop at 24fps contains 120 frames; at 30fps it contains 150. Avoid a duplicate endpoint frame causing a perceptible hold.
- Prefer a periodic reflection sweep while the line stays geometrically fixed. A one-way bright line that abruptly teleports back is not a seamless loop.
- If a generator cannot produce continuity, edit a short dissolve only where the prism is stationary. Reject ghosted edges. A deterministic rendered loop or a static poster is better than a visible seam.
- Never promise “perfect loop” until the exported result has been checked.

## 11. Motion design language

Hero:

- One ambient loop.
- One entrance reveal.
- One slow signal-line movement.
- One restrained scroll exit.

Below the fold:

- Opacity and 12–16px translate reveals.
- 40–80ms stagger between sibling items.
- 2–4px hover lift on fine pointers.
- No perpetual motion on every card.

Page transitions:

- Keep the existing iris transition.
- Use a terracotta ring and charcoal curtain.
- Desktop fine pointer: 350–500ms.
- Mobile or reduced motion: fade or instant transition.

Constraints:

- Animate `transform` and `opacity` wherever possible.
- Do not animate width, height, margin, or padding.
- Pause videos and loops when offscreen or when the document is hidden.
- A 5-second clip looping indefinitely is motion lasting more than five seconds: provide a visible keyboard-accessible Pause animation control. Reduced-motion detection alone does not replace that control.
- Do not separately animate the signal line if it is already moving in the video. Keep at most one ambient moving layer in the viewport.
- Do not run GSAP or persistent requestAnimationFrame loops on mobile.
- Use `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` to render the final state directly.
- Use `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` to replace blur with opaque surfaces.

## 12. Web delivery specifications

### Images

- Source master: at least 2× the rendered dimensions.
- AVIF where supported, WebP fallback, JPEG only as legacy fallback.
- Hero poster target: under 220KB.
- Below-fold image target: under 180KB each.
- Transparent object target: under 160KB each.
- Always include explicit `width` and `height`.
- Eager-load only the first hero visual or poster.
- Lazy-load below-fold media.
- Use `decoding="async"` for non-critical images.
- Use `sizes` and `srcset` when multiple raster widths exist.

### Video

- Desktop WebM (VP9 or AV1): target 1.5–3MB for a 5-second hero loop.
- MP4 fallback: H.264, `yuv420p`, fast-start enabled; target under 4MB.
- Mobile video: target under 1.2MB or use the poster only.
- Decorative playback uses `muted`, `playsinline`, and `loop`; request autoplay only after eligibility checks. Browser autoplay can still fail, so retain the poster.
- No audio tracks.
- The poster must communicate the visual without video playback.
- AV1 is optional, not inherently faster: check decode support and smoothness on intended hardware. A lighter VP9/H.264 encode can be preferable.
- Aim for one hero poster request before first paint, not multiple eagerly loaded object plates. Size budgets are production targets, not measured results.

### Safe play-on-demand markup

This is the no-script accessible version, not an ambient-autoplay implementation. Use after the listed files exist. Native controls let users start, pause, and stop without a custom controller.

```html
<video
  class="hero-ambient-loop"
  controls
  muted
  loop
  playsinline
  preload="none"
  width="1920"
  height="1080"
  poster="/assets/visual/hero/hero-ambient-poster.webp"
  aria-label="NextReach glass artwork animation"
>
  <source src="/assets/visual/hero/hero-ambient.av1.webm" type="video/webm" />
  <source src="/assets/visual/hero/hero-ambient.mp4" type="video/mp4" />
</video>
```

The headline, paragraph, CTA, and accessibility description must remain HTML and must still work when video fails to load.

For ambient mode, render the static poster first. Attach video sources only for fine-pointer desktop devices without reduced motion or a data-saving preference. Hiding a `<video>` with CSS does not reliably prevent downloading its sources. The controller must catch playback rejection, preserve the poster, pause on `visibilitychange` and offscreen observation, obey the visitor's pause choice, and remove listeners on navigation. Use `aria-hidden="true"` only on the decorative media, never on its accessible Pause control.

## 14. Production workflow

### Step 1: Generate concepts

Generate three variations for A01, A03, A07, A08, A09, and A10. Keep camera, material, light, and palette fixed. Choose one base family before generating the rest.

### Step 2: Lock the material family

Select the version with the clearest silhouette, least visual noise, strongest terracotta signal line, most natural translucent material, and enough negative space for HTML content.

Do not mix objects from different generation families without matching them in a design tool.

### Step 3: Capture real product media

Record the real case-study flows. Clean all personal, private, and unpublished information before export.

### Step 4: Build poster frames

Every video gets a poster with the same crop, color grade, and contrast. Posters must be good enough to ship alone.

### Step 5: Optimize and integrate

Convert, compress, resize, and inspect every asset at its actual rendered size. Keep important text, labels, buttons, metrics, and accessibility content in Astro/HTML.

### Step 6: Test motion states

Test desktop fine pointer, 375px mobile, 768px tablet, 1024px laptop, 1440px desktop, reduced motion, reduced transparency, slow network, disabled autoplay, and dark-hero/light-page transitions.
