# Owner interview and offer-validation guide

**Internal working guide — 9 October 2026.** Based on the [final decision memo](../nextreach-final-decision-memo-2026-10-09.md). No conversations, offers, deposits or results are implied by this template.

## Start here

1. Copy [the blank workbook](validation-tracker.xlsx) to a private location outside the repository **before adding real information**. Keep this repository version blank. Do not paste contact details, private quotations, payment references or client records into git.
2. Read the workbook's **Start** sheet, choose the batch start and review dates in **Settings**, and use one Lead ID per business. The prefilled IDs are empty slots, not researched leads.
3. Start with owner-managed machining suppliers in Chakan–Pimpri-Chinchwad. An old website is a candidate signal, not proof of a problem. Include shops with no urgent need in the record instead of silently removing inconvenient evidence.
4. Use appropriate business contact routes or introductions. Ask permission for the conversation and any follow-up; respect refusals and do-not-contact requests. No automatic follow-up sequence is created by this kit.
5. Conduct discovery before showing the [scope sheet](capability-website-scope.md). Do not promise a start slot or collect payment until a written quote addresses fit, taxes, third-party costs and terms.

The scope sheet is the only buyer-facing artifact here. Do not send the internal workbook or this guide to prospects. There is no demo asset in this kit: use an honestly labelled concept if one is later prepared, or record that the demo portion of the test is still missing. Do not present unrelated work as a machining-client result.

## Part 1 — Neutral discovery, 12–15 minutes

### Permission and disclosure — 1 minute

> “I'm Rajesh from NextReach Studio. I build websites and am testing a focused service for machining businesses. Before suggesting anything, I'd like to understand how new buyers assess your capabilities today. Could we spend about 12 minutes on that? There is no obligation to buy. Is it okay if I take brief notes?”

Do not record audio without separate permission. Do not ask for confidential drawings, customer identities, private quotes or credentials. A description of the process is enough.

### Last real buyer evaluation — 4 minutes

- “Think about the last new buyer who checked whether you could do a job. How did they find you, and what happened next?”
- “What information did they ask for? How did you provide it?”
- “Was anything difficult or delayed? If not, what worked well?”
- If a difficulty is mentioned: “Can you describe that specific occasion? What happened as a result?”

Follow the owner's example. Do not introduce equipment lists, certifications or websites as assumed problems. Do not translate an inconvenience into lost revenue unless the owner has evidence.

### Current alternatives and priority — 3 minutes

- “What do you use today to explain your capabilities to a new buyer?”
- “What works well enough that you would keep it?”
- “Have you tried changing any part of this? What happened?”
- “Compared with your other business priorities, how important is changing this now?”

A brochure, direct phone call or existing website may already solve the job. Record that honestly.

### Trigger and buying process — 3 minutes

- “Is anything happening in the next three months that changes how you need to approach buyers?”
- If yes: “What is it, and approximately when?”
- “Who would decide whether to change how you present the business? Would anyone else approve the content or spending?”
- “If you did decide to update your materials, who could provide approved technical facts and photos?”

Do not count general ambition to grow as a dated trigger. Do not ask whether ₹18,000 is affordable to decide if someone belongs in the offer test.

### Reflect and close — 2 minutes

> “What I heard is [their actual problem], currently handled through [their current method], with [their actual trigger, or no current trigger]. Have I understood that correctly?”

Then ask one appropriate next step:

- **Relevant problem and readiness:** “Would you like to see a fixed-scope option, either now or at a time you choose?”
- **No present fit:** “Thank you—that helps. I won't recommend changing what already works. Would you prefer no follow-up?”
- **Unknown authority or inputs:** “What would need to be clarified before it would be useful to discuss an option?”

No pitch is required when there is no fit.

## Record evidence, not enthusiasm

Complete the private **Leads** row immediately after the conversation. Use `Yes`, `No` or `Unknown` consistently; blank means not recorded, not a negative answer.

| Field | Count it only when |
|---|---|
| Substantive owner conversation | An owner/approver discussed a real evaluation or the actual current process; a greeting or chat response is insufficient |
| Concrete information problem | A specific incident or recurring task was described; “a better website would be nice” is insufficient |
| Active trigger within 90 days | A real upcoming action/event/capability and approximate timing were named |
| Feasible inputs | A responsible person can provide permitted, approved technical material |
| Eligible offer | Need, decision authority, timing and feasible inputs are documented—**not** prior acceptance of the price |

Separate the owner's words from your interpretation in private notes. Record disagreement, absence of urgency and successful alternatives. One business counts once, even if several people or repeat calls are involved.

## Part 2 — Offer discussion, only with permission

### Explain the proposed fit

> “You said [their confirmed issue]. The option I'm testing is a five-page capability website: Home, Capabilities, Equipment & Quality, About, and Contact/Request a Quote. The pilot service fee is ₹18,000. It includes one content interview, bounded content editing and two review rounds. It helps present approved information; it does not promise leads or sales.”

Walk through the scope sheet, including exclusions, taxes, client-paid domain/hosting and the ten-working-day target after complete inputs and a confirmed slot. Do not conceal limits to obtain a deposit.

### Use the same honestly labelled example

If a concept is available, introduce it as **“Demonstration concept—not a delivered machining-client project or evidence of results.”** Show how a buyer would find capability information and request a quotation. Use no invented customer logos or certification claims.

Ask: “In your words, what would this help with, if anything? What is missing for your buyers? What do you think is included, and what would happen next?” Record confusion and objections before explaining further. The workbook only counts the standardized offer test when an example was actually shown; a scope-only discussion remains useful discovery, not a completed version of Experiment B.

### Ask for a real decision, not a compliment

> “If the scope fits, I can prepare a written quote and confirm a start slot. Booking would require a ₹9,000 service-fee deposit, plus any applicable tax disclosed in the quote. The other ₹9,000 is due after the approved preview and before launch or handover. Is there a specific concern you would need resolved before deciding?”

This is not an instruction to pay immediately. Confirm the responsible approver, inputs, obligations and written terms first. Do not invent scarcity, offer unpriced extras, or negotiate away the test price to improve the count.

| Response | Neutral follow-up |
|---|---|
| “Too expensive” | “What are you comparing it with, and which part does not justify the cost for you?” |
| “We need guaranteed enquiries” | “I cannot guarantee enquiries. This scope improves presentation and contact access; it is not an acquisition campaign.” |
| “Our current method works” | “What, if anything, would need to change for this to become a priority?” |
| “Show me industrial results” | “I can show only work and results I can substantiate. A concept is not client proof. What evidence would you need?” |
| “We need ERP, uploads or another major feature” | “That is outside this scope. I can record the requirement; I won't include it without a separate agreed scope and price.” |
| “Send something” | “May I send this scope? When, if at all, would a follow-up be useful?” |

Record an explicit next step and permitted follow-up date. Respect a no. A pending decision is not a win; a verbal yes is not a deposit. Record refunds as well as receipts.

## Run the three tests without moving the goalposts

| Test | Bound | Continue signal | If not met |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Access and urgency** | First ten working days; up to 30 shops; ten prospecting hours | Six substantive owner conversations; at least three with both a concrete problem and an active trigger | Distinguish inaccessible owners from no urgency. Stop extra industrial brand investment; clinics become the next sequential test, not a parallel campaign. |
| **B — Willingness to pay** | First 30 days; eight distinct eligible decision-makers shown the same scope and labelled example | At least two net ₹9,000 service-fee deposits at the ₹18,000 fee, without concessions or hidden extras | Under eight offers means unresolved access. Zero/one deposit after eight offers means revise the evidenced value/proof issue before another bounded test—not automatic discounting. |
| **C — Delivery economics** | First two paid pilots, including the reconciled 30-day defect window | Both accepted; enquiry receipt and handover checked; each ≤20 all-in hours and ≥₹800/hour cash contribution before founder pay/overhead | Stop scaling, correct scope/method and recost. Reject the model if repeat jobs cannot meet the floor at a price buyers accept. |

These are management guardrails, not statistical significance or market benchmarks. Do not call the model proven because two buyers paid. Do not treat a missed deadline with an inadequate sample as a definitive price rejection.

### Workbook rules that prevent misleading economics

- **Offers:** Eight slots represent eight distinct businesses, not repeated proposal versions. `Eligible?` requires the discovery evidence above. Enter the actual fee and disclosures; only unchanged, fully explained ₹18,000 offers with an example count toward B.
- **Cash:** Enter amounts as positive numbers and use exactly `Fee received`, `Fee refund` or `External cost`. Receipts/refunds use the matching Lead ID. Exclude tax collected for remittance and client-paid hosting/domain pass-throughs; this is a service cash-contribution tracker, not statutory accounts. Refunds reduce the deposit count.
- **Time:** Record actual decimal hours, including unsuccessful selling. Stage `A` covers the access test; `B` covers later sales work; `Delivery` covers pilot fulfilment. Use category `Prospecting` for research/initial contact effort. Use other clear categories for interviews/selling, content, build, revisions, QA, administration and support.
- **Shared allocation:** Log lead-specific time/cost against that Lead ID and general work against `POOL`. The workbook allocates all remaining batch time/cost equally across the populated pilot slots after subtracting their direct work. Do not copy pooled work into each pilot manually. With only one pilot assigned, it bears the entire pool; results can change when the second is assigned.
- **Batch boundary:** Use one workbook per experiment. Include all acquisition work for this batch, but only fulfilment for its first two pilots. Do not mix other projects, later cohorts, or the ₹5,000 exception into this workbook. Fix the pool boundary at the B review and document it privately.
- **Support:** Enter only *remaining estimated* support hours in Pilots H. Actual support goes in Time. Reduce the reserve as support is logged; do not count the same hours twice. Set H to numeric `0` only when no reserve remains. Results remain provisional until the defect window is reconciled.
- **Completion:** `All batch records complete? = Yes` means the owner has checked all collections/refunds, costs and hours, unique IDs, dates, category spelling, and allocation completeness. Zero costs must be verified, not assumed because the ledger is empty. Both pilots also need full service-fee collection, acceptance, the enquiry/handover check and reconciled support before a final positive C indicator.
- **Limits:** Leads has 30 slots, Offers has eight, Pilots has two. Time/Cash formulas cover rows 2–501 only. Do not add data beyond those limits without extending every affected formula. Cells are not locked and there are no enforced dropdowns: exact labels and manual input review matter. Open in Excel or another compatible spreadsheet app and recalculate before relying on indicators.
