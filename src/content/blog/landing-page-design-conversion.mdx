A landing page has one job: convert a visitor who arrived from a specific source into a specific action. Everything else on the page is in service of that.

Most Pune landing pages fail not from bad design but from trying to serve too many audiences at once - a homepage that is also a landing page is the classic mistake.

## The First Question: Is This Actually a Landing Page?

A landing page answers to a single source and a single action.

| Page type | Single source? | Single action? | Valid as landing page? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | No | No | No |
| Google Ads landing page | Yes | Should be | Yes |
| Campaign page for a service | Yes | Should be | Yes |
| Blog post | Sometimes | Should be | Sometimes |
| Product page | Yes | Add to cart | Yes |
| Service page | Mixed | Contact | Partially |

**The common mistake:** sending every ad click, every WhatsApp message, and every referral to your homepage. The visitor has no context, so they read everything and do nothing.

If you are running any paid or outbound campaign - Google Ads, Meta, WhatsApp outreach, a LinkedIn post - give it a dedicated page. One promise, one action, one audience.

## The Structure That Works

This is the skeleton. It is not decoration - each section exists because it answers a question the visitor is asking in sequence.

**1. Hero: what is this and who is it for**

Within the first screen, three things must be clear: what you offer, who it is for, and what to do next. If any of the three is missing, they leave.

The test: show the hero to someone unfamiliar with your business and ask what you do. If they cannot answer in five seconds, rewrite it.

**2. Proof that you are real**

This belongs high. Pune buyers are cautious about spending money online, particularly on businesses they cannot visit. Reviews, client names, numbers, certifications, and a real address all belong near the top - not buried in a footer.

The 2026 design consensus has moved decisively here. Generic stock photography of people pointing at laptops actively reduces trust for local services, because it looks like exactly what it is. Real photographs of your actual work, your actual team, or your actual premises outperform polished stock imagery by a wide margin.

**3. The specific problem, in their words**

Say the thing your customer is actually worried about, using the language they would use. Not "we leverage synergistic solutions to empower your workflow." Say "your dispatchers spend 90 minutes every morning assigning drivers by hand."

**4. What you do about it**

Concrete deliverables, not capabilities. "We built a dispatch system that assigns drivers automatically based on location and delivery priority" beats "we deliver innovative logistics solutions."

**5. Proof it worked**

A case study with a number in it. Not a testimonial without context - "Great work, very professional." Instead: "Dispatch time dropped from 90 minutes to 5. Same-day delivery rate up 18%."

Specific numbers are also what AI answer engines cite. This one section does double duty for SEO.

**6. Objection handling**

The real reasons people do not buy, answered honestly. Cost, timeline, lock-in, what happens if it does not work. If you do not address these, you are assuming the objection does not exist.

**7. Process**

How it works, how long, what they must do. Reduces the perceived effort of contacting you more than any amount of persuasion copy.

**8. FAQ**

Real questions, in the customer's language, answered in two or three sentences each. This section is disproportionately effective for both conversion and AI citation.

**9. One clear call to action**

Repeated, consistent, and singular. Not "Book a call" and "WhatsApp us" and "Fill the form" competing for attention. Pick the primary action and make it the same every time.

## What Indian Buyers Specifically Need

These are not universal conversion principles. These are what Pune businesses converting on WhatsApp require.

**A WhatsApp path.** For Indian SMEs, WhatsApp is the primary business communication channel. A tap-to-chat link that opens a pre-filled message is frequently the highest-converting element on the page.

**Price transparency, at least a range.** Hiding all pricing filters out the customers you want and wastes your time on the ones who were never going to buy. Our own model publishes ranges on every service page for exactly this reason.

**Local credibility.** A real Pune address. A Google Maps embed. Names of areas you serve. Local phone number in a click-to-call element. Local proof outranks national brands for local intent.

**Plain language.** Plain language helps non-native English speakers and busy readers - and it is also how people search. No jargon.

**UPI for payments.** Where payment is collected at all, UPI is expected.

## Design Decisions That Quietly Lose Leads

**Hero animations that delay the message.** Animation that runs for two seconds before text appears means two seconds of nothing to say. If the first meaningful paint is delayed, you lose people who never wait to find out what it was.

**Below-16px body text.** Unreadable on a phone without zooming, and a WCAG failure at AA.

**Tap targets under 24x24 pixels.** WCAG 2.2 Level AA minimum. This is the most common mobile conversion killer we encounter.

**Forms asking for too much.** Every field reduces completion rate. Ask only what you need to take the next step. A phone number and a one-line "what do you need" usually beats a twelve-field enquiry form.

**Contact forms that do not confirm.** If someone submits and sees nothing happen, they assume it failed and they leave. Send a confirmation immediately, to them and to your phone.

**No mobile menu, or a broken one.** Roughly 80% of Indian traffic is mobile. A hamburger that opens a full-screen overlay with no visible close button is a total conversion loss on mobile.

**Slow pages.** Widely cited research puts mobile abandonment at 53% when loading exceeds three seconds, and conversion loss around 7% per additional second. Over 70% of top-500 sites load slower on mobile than desktop. Your landing page should be the fastest page you own.

**Generic stock imagery.** Covered above. It signals "this is a template" and it is correct.

## Speed Is a Conversion Feature

This gets treated as an SEO topic when it is equally a conversion topic.

Field data across 37,036 websites found 52% of page-one Google results fail Core Web Vitals, with 67% of failures driven by Largest Contentful Paint. The median web page weighs 2.6MB across 71 requests.

For a landing page specifically:

- Ship as little JavaScript as possible. Astro produces static HTML with zero JavaScript by default; a page with one React island is around 14KB.
- Optimise the hero image first. It is almost always the LCP element.
- Self-host fonts, and load them non-render-blocking.
- Lazy-load everything below the fold.
- Have a real TTFB - under 800ms. Cheap shared hosting frequently cannot do this.

A landing page is the one page on your site where speed effort has a direct, measurable revenue return.

## How to Test a Landing Page Honestly

**The five-second test.** Show the page to five people unfamiliar with your business for five seconds. Ask what you do and who you serve. If fewer than four out of five get both right, your hero is failing.

**The mobile walk.** Open it on your own phone on mobile data. Time the load. Try to complete the primary action. Then try with one hand.

**The objection test.** Write down the three reasons a real customer would not buy from you. Then check whether the page addresses all three. Most will not.

**The source test.** Visit the page from the exact ad, post, or message that would send traffic there. If the visitor arrives with expectations the page does not immediately confirm, they leave.

**The form test.** Submit it on mobile. Did you get the confirmation? Did your phone ring?

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long should a landing page be?**
Long enough to answer every question that blocks the action, and no longer. For a ₹30,000-₹60,000 Pune service purchase, that is usually 600-1,200 words plus proof. For a low-value impulse purchase, 200 words. Testimonials and FAQ add length without harming conversion.

**How many calls to action should a landing page have?**
One primary action, repeated. Competing CTAs - "book a call," "chat on WhatsApp," "fill the form" - split attention and reduce total conversions. If you genuinely need WhatsApp and a form, pick the primary and make the secondary visually secondary.

**Does landing page design affect SEO?**
Indirectly but meaningfully. Landing pages are often the destination for paid campaigns, and Quality Score is affected by landing page experience. They also frequently target specific keywords, where content quality and structure both matter. Our [SEO checklist](/blog/seo-friendly-website-checklist) covers the technical side.

**Should landing pages be indexed by Google?**
Usually yes - they often target specific service or location keywords. Exception: campaign-specific ad landing pages with no search intent, which you should `noindex`.

**What is the average conversion rate for a landing page?**
Any specific figure is marketing. Measure your own baseline before changing anything, then measure again after. Without a baseline you cannot prove an improvement, and this is why most Pune landing page redesigns cannot be evaluated.

**Is it worth A/B testing a landing page?**
Only after you have enough traffic. Below roughly 1,000 visits per month, tests will not reach significance and you will make decisions on noise. At that traffic level, spend effort on clarity and speed instead.
