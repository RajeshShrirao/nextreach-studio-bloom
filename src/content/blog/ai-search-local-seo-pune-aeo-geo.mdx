AI Overviews now appear on more than 40% of local business queries. Google AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly active users within a year of launch, and AI Overviews passed 2.5 billion. For a Pune business, the practical effect is that a meaningful share of your potential customers now see a composed answer - naming three or four businesses - instead of ten blue links they might click.

This guide covers what actually changed, what Google says officially, and what to do about it.

## First, the Official Position

Google published a dedicated AI Search optimisation guide in May 2026, and it is more conservative than most of the industry advice floating around.

Google's position, in its own framing:

> "From Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO."

The guide also explicitly names tactics site owners can skip for Google Search:
- **llms.txt is not required.** Google says you do not need AI text files, special markup, or Markdown files to appear in generative AI search.
- **There is no special schema.** Structured data remains useful for rich results, but no schema.org type is privileged for AI visibility.
- **Content chunking is not a requirement.**

This matters because a lot of paid "GEO" advice is selling panic. Google is telling you the fundamentals still hold. What changed is the format of what gets surfaced, not the ranking system underneath.

**Where Google is leaving ambiguity is the opportunity.** OpenAI, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini all cite sources independently of Google. Adoption of `llms.txt` - a plain markdown index at your site root, the proposed standard from llmstxt.org - reached roughly 844,000 websites by early 2026. That is still a tiny slice of the web, which means deploying it now is cheap and defensible.

NextReach ships `/llms.txt` and a machine-readable `/pricing-for-agents.md`, and explicitly allows 15 AI crawlers including GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and Google-Extended. Very few Pune competitors do this.

## What Actually Gets You Cited

### 1. Lead with the answer

Analysis of 1,000 pages that were frequently cited by AI assistants found consistent structural patterns: short paragraphs averaging about three sentences, heavy use of lists, and explicit question-and-answer formatting.

The pattern is simple. Open each section with the conclusion, then support it. If someone scans your page and has to reconstruct your answer, the answer does not get quoted.

### 2. Use specific numbers with attribution

"Most businesses" is vague. "52% of page-one Google results fail Core Web Vitals" is extractable. Generative engines cite specifics with sources. If you have proprietary data - your own delivery times, your own cost figures, your own before-and-after metrics - that data is the hardest thing for a competitor to replicate and the easiest thing for an engine to cite.

### 3. Build topic clusters, not scattered posts

Service pages, FAQs, case studies, and articles should link to each other deliberately. An AI engine trying to establish what a business does reads a cluster, not an orphan page. Your AI automation service page, the Pune AI automation article, and a manufacturing-industry page should form a connected set around one subject.

### 4. Be consistent everywhere

For local businesses specifically, this is the highest-leverage item. Your name, address, and phone number must match exactly across:
- Your website
- Your Google Business Profile
- Justdial, Sulekha, IndiaMART
- Your social profiles

Inconsistency does not just hurt classic local SEO. It actively feeds AI hallucination about your business. Research on local AI answers found clear accuracy correlations, and businesses are already reporting incorrect hours or wrong ratings surfacing in AI Mode summaries.

### 5. Mark up your structured data

Correct schema does not rank you on its own. It reduces the cost of a machine understanding your entity, which makes citation more likely. For a Pune business the baseline is `LocalBusiness` with your exact NAP and coordinates, `Service` on each service page, `FAQPage` where you genuinely answer questions, and `Article` plus `BreadcrumbList` on editorial content. ## The Local AI Visibility Stack

Research on Google AI Overviews and AI Mode for local queries surfaces a consistent four-signal pattern. Miss any one and combined visibility collapses.

| Signal | What it means | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Complete Google Business Profile | Primary category, services, attributes, hours, photos, recent posts | Generic category, or no posts in 6+ months |
| Anchor reviews | Strong Google reviews plus presence on one vertical directory | Relying only on Google, no directory presence |
| Schema-marked services | Service schema matching actual offered services | Schema on the homepage only |
| Third-party citations | Mentions in publications Google already trusts for local queries | Zero off-site corroboration |

Two findings worth internalising:

**AI Mode mostly reads your Google Business Profile, not your website.** For high-intent queries like "best [service] near me," AI Mode is largely handing back Google's own Maps data. That means GBP work is not optional if you want AI visibility.

**AI Overviews read your website.** AI Overviews behaves like a search engine. It rewards technically sound, content-rich sites. Steady Demand's analysis of the two surfaces puts it bluntly: you are running two campaigns, a content campaign for AI Overviews and a Business Profile campaign for AI Mode, and most local businesses fund only one.

A study of 9,435 Google AI Mode answers to home-services questions found the median named business had a 4.9-star rating and 342 reviews. Businesses named first had a median 342 reviews versus 195 for those named third. Only 3.7% of rated business cards named any business under 4.0 stars.

## What to Do First: A Practical Order

**Week 1 - Fix the factual base.** Audit your Business Profile and every directory listing. Make NAP identical everywhere. Confirm hours, category, and service area are accurate. This is unglamorous and it is the foundation for both AI surfaces.

**Week 2 - Restructure your highest-value pages.** Take your three most commercially important pages. Open every section with a direct answer. Break paragraphs to roughly three sentences. Add a real FAQ with actual questions your customers ask. Convert vague claims into specific numbers.

**Week 3 - Add the markup.** LocalBusiness and Service schema. Validate with Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema Markup Validator. Do not skip validation - malformed markup gets ignored wholesale rather than partially read.

**Week 4 - Ship llms.txt.** Write a short markdown index at your site root: what the business does, who it serves, the key pages, and the services. Include prices if you are willing to publish them. It costs an afternoon.

**Ongoing - Ask, do not assume.** Run your real customer questions through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Mode each month. Record what they say and which sources they cite. This is the only reliable measurement of AI visibility, and it takes about twenty minutes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is GEO a replacement for SEO?**
No. Google explicitly frames it as still SEO. GEO is an extension focused on being cited inside generative answers. The foundations - crawlability, speed, clear entity definitions, real content - are shared. If your basic SEO is weak, no amount of AI optimisation will fix it.

**Do I need llms.txt if Google says it doesn't?**
Not for Google. Consider it for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini, which make their own citation choices. It is an afternoon of work, adoption is still low, and it costs nothing to have. Treat it as cheap optionality, not a ranking lever.

**Does FAQPage schema still work?**
Changed in 2026. Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in Search as of 7 May 2026. The markup is still worth deploying because answer engines ingest question-and-answer pairs cleanly, and because the FAQ text itself is what gets quoted. Just do not expect a visual rich result in Google any more.

**How long does it take to appear in AI answers?**
There is no guaranteed timeline and no one can buy placement. What moves fastest is factual consistency and specific content. Businesses that fixed NAP and published specific, structured pages started seeing citations within weeks. Businesses that only published blog posts saw slower change.

**Can I pay to appear in an AI Overview?**
No. There is no paid placement mechanism. Anyone offering guaranteed AI placement is selling something that does not exist.

**How is this different for a Pune B2B business versus a consumer one?**
Mostly the review-volume component. Consumer local search leans heavily on ratings and Google Business Profile. B2B leans more on cited expertise, specific case data, and third-party mentions. The technical work - schema, structure, answer-first formatting, llms.txt - is identical.
