Most Pune website projects do not fail because of the technology. They fail because nobody agreed what "done" means, so everyone discovered the gaps late and paid for them.

This guide covers the actual process, what you receive at each stage, realistic timelines, and how to keep a project on track.

## The Five Stages

| Stage | Duration (6-12 page site) | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery | 2-5 days | Scope document, requirements, technical plan |
| 2. Wireframes | 3-7 days | Low-fidelity structure for every page |
| 3. Design | 1-2 weeks | High-fidelity mockups, mobile + desktop, all states |
| 4. Build | 2-4 weeks | Working site, CMS, integrations, staging |
| 5. QA and launch | 3-5 days | Testing, redirects, launch, monitoring |

Total: **4-8 weeks** for a professional business site. Anyone promising 7 days for design and build of the same scope is producing a template.

## Stage 1: Discovery

This is where projects are won or lost, and it is the stage most often skipped.

**What should be covered:**

- **What does the business actually do, and how does it make money?** Not the marketing version. The operational version.
- **Who are the customers and what are they worried about?** If nobody asked, the site will be decoration.
- **What is the primary action?** Contact, book, buy, download. One, prioritized.
- **What must the site do?** Login, database, booking, payment, integration.
- **What content exists?** Photography, copy, documents. This is frequently the real schedule risk.
- **What is the technical reality?** Existing site, domain, hosting, analytics, CRM, phone system.
- **What is the budget and timeline?** Stated honestly at the start, not discovered in week four.

**Deliverable:** a written scope document listing every page, every feature, and every explicit exclusion. If it is not written down, it is not agreed.

The exclusion list matters most. "Out of scope: multilingual, CMS beyond text fields, third-party integration beyond the contact form" prevents the three most common late arguments.

## Stage 2: Wireframes

Low-fidelity structure for every page - boxes and labels, no styling.

**This is the cheapest stage and the most valuable.** Structural problems found here cost days to fix. The same problems found after development cost weeks.

**What to review:** Is the phone number reachable? Is the primary action obvious? Does the structure match what customers search for? Is anything missing that your customers always ask?

Most Pune business owners skip this and go straight to visual design. That is backwards - you end up polishing a structure that does not work.

**Time: 3-7 days for a 6-12 page site.**

## Stage 3: Design

High-fidelity visual design, desktop and mobile, **plus every state**.

**The states nobody draws, and everyone pays for:**

- Form empty, filling, submitting, error, success
- Page loading
- Content empty (no blog posts yet, no products yet)
- Error (404, 500, form failure)
- Long content (a 500-word service description breaking the layout)

Each of these is real work. Each is routinely unquoted.

**Also required:**

- **WCAG 2.2 AA compliance spec** - tap targets at 24x24px minimum, 4.5:1 contrast, visible focus, focus not obscured by sticky headers
- **A design system** - type scale, colour tokens, spacing, components. Without this, development guesses and the site drifts
- **Mobile design first**, not a desktop layout squeezed down

**Time: 1-2 weeks.**

## Stage 4: Build

The engineering stage. What should be happening:

**In parallel:** frontend, backend, CMS setup, integrations, and QA are not sequential phases. They overlap. If your vendor builds everything then tests, you have a slower project and later surprises.

**In sprints.** Every two weeks you see working software. This is the single most important structural protection for a client, because it converts "I hope this is right" into "I can see it is right."

**What should exist at the end:**

- Working site on real infrastructure, staging URL live
- CMS for content, if in scope
- All integrations working - forms, WhatsApp, payments, CRM
- Structured data implemented and validated
- Core Web Vitals measured on a mid-range Android
- Source code in a repository you own

**Time: 2-4 weeks for a 6-12 page site.**

## Stage 5: QA and Launch

The stage that gets compressed, and the cause of most post-launch problems.

**Functional QA:** every link, every form, every navigation path, on mobile and desktop. Test on real devices, not browser resize.

**Accessibility QA:** keyboard navigation, contrast, tap targets, screen reader labels, focus order.

**Performance QA:** Core Web Vitals on real hardware. Field data, not just lab scores.

**SEO QA:** title tags, meta descriptions, canonicals, sitemap, robots.txt, structured data validated with Google's tools.

**Launch QA:** redirect map applied, analytics installed, SSL working, backups verified, mobile testing on a real phone.

**Time: 3-5 days.**

**Content is the most common schedule risk.** If copy and photography are not ready at week one, everything downstream waits.

## How to Keep a Project on Track

**1. Insist on a written scope with an explicit exclusion list.**
Most disputes trace back to something nobody wrote down or something assumed was included.

**2. Review at every stage, not just the end.**
Wireframes, then design, then staging. Catching a structural problem in week one costs a day. Catching it in week six costs a week.

**3. Set a change-request process.**
Every post-signoff change is a written change order with a cost and a timeline. "Unlimited revisions" is not a benefit - it means nobody agreed what finished looks like.

**4. Demand a staging URL from week one of the build.**
Not a preview at the end. A live staging environment you can check continuously.

**5. Own the content.** Provide copy and images on schedule. The most common delay in Pune projects is waiting on client content, and it is entirely avoidable.

**6. Pay in stages.** Never more than 30-40% upfront. Never 100% before launch.

**7. Confirm code ownership in writing.** A repository you can clone, or documented database and content exports.

## Questions to Ask at Every Stage

**At discovery:** "What is out of scope?"
**At wireframes:** "Does this answer the questions customers actually ask?"
**At design:** "Have you designed the error, empty, and loading states?"
**At build:** "Can I see the staging URL today?"
**Before launch:** "What are the Core Web Vitals on a mid-range Android?"
**After launch:** "What does post-launch support include, and for how long?"

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does website development take in Pune?**
5-8 weeks for a professional 6-12 page business site. 1 week for a landing page. 4-6 weeks for a simple e-commerce store. 3-6 months for a custom web application.

**How much does website development cost in Pune?**
₹30,000-₹60,000 for a professional business site. ₹8,000-₹25,000 for a landing page. ₹35,000-₹90,000 for an e-commerce store. ₹1,50,000+ for a web application. Our [full cost breakdown](/blog/website-design-cost-pune-2026) covers all tiers.

**Do I need wireframes before design?**
Yes, for anything beyond a landing page. They are the cheapest stage and catch structural problems while they are still cheap to fix.

**How involved should I be in the process?**
Enough to answer questions and review at each stage. Not enough to make design decisions daily. The two failures we see are disengagement (which produces a site nobody approved) and micromanagement (which produces scope changes nobody priced).

**What happens if I need changes after the design is approved?**
That is a change request. It should be written, costed, and scheduled. A good vendor has a rate for it. If changes are free and unlimited, either the project is small or nobody defined "done."

**Who should own the website - me or the agency?**
You. Domain in a company email account, code in a repository you control, hosting with credentials you hold. If the vendor cannot hand over all three, they are not building your asset.

**How do I know the site is actually ready to launch?**
Every link tested, every form tested on mobile, Core Web Vitals measured on real hardware, accessibility checked, structured data validated with Google's tools, redirects mapped, analytics installed, backups verified. See our [SEO checklist](/blog/seo-friendly-website-checklist) for the full list.
