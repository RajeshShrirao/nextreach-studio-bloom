If you run a business in Pune and you're reading this, you've probably already discovered the first problem: there are hundreds of agencies claiming to build software, and most of them are WordPress theme shops wearing a tech disguise.

We hear from business owners every week who spent ₹3-5 lakhs on a "custom web application" only to receive a WordPress site with a page builder and a sluggish plugin stack. The agency called it custom. The business owner paid for custom. But what they got was something they could have built themselves on Wix.

This guide is designed to help you avoid that outcome. Whether you need a customer portal, an internal operations dashboard, or a full ERP system, the framework below will help you separate genuine engineering firms from resellers and theme-shop agencies.

## Why Most Pune Agencies Can't Build What You Need

Pune's IT services ecosystem grew up around staffing and enterprise outsourcing. The typical agency model looks like this:

- A salesperson (who does not write code) sells you a project
- A project manager (who also does not write code) scopes it
- A team of 0-2 year junior developers builds it
- You pay for the salesperson's commission, the PM's salary, the office rent, and the juniors' learning curve

The result is that 60-70% of what you pay goes to non-development overhead. The developers who actually touch your code are the cheapest and least experienced people in the chain.

This model works fine for large enterprises with dedicated vendor management teams. For a Pune SME paying out of pocket? It's a disaster.

## Three Red Flags to Watch For

### Red Flag 1: You Can't Talk to the Developers

If every call goes through an account manager or a project manager, that's a problem. The people selling you the work are not the people building it. They will promise features that are difficult or expensive to build. They will underestimate timelines because they don't understand the technical complexity.

A good agency gives you direct access to the senior engineer who will architect and review your project. You should be able to ask technical questions and get technical answers.

### Red Flag 2: "We'll Figure Out Pricing Later"

Some agencies refuse to give fixed-price quotes. They want to start with a "discovery phase" that costs ₹50,000-1,00,000 before they'll tell you what the project will cost.

This is not always bad - complex projects genuinely need discovery. But if the agency cannot give you a rough range (e.g., "projects like this typically run ₹3-5 lakhs"), they either lack experience with your type of project or they plan to upsell you repeatedly once you're committed.

### Red Flag 3: Junior-Heavy Teams with No Senior Oversight

Ask directly: who will architect the system? Who will review the pull requests? Who handles deployment and infrastructure?

If the answer is "our team of React developers," dig deeper. Junior developers can execute tasks, but they cannot make good architectural decisions. Without senior engineering oversight, you will accumulate technical debt that makes future changes expensive and risky.

## What to Look For Instead

### Direct Access to Senior Engineers

The agency should be able to introduce you to the person who will lead your project's technical architecture. This person should have 8+ years of experience, should be able to discuss trade-offs (why PostgreSQL over MySQL, why Astro over Next.js), and should be available throughout the project.

### Fixed-Scope, Fixed-Price Proposals

A good agency can scope a well-defined project and give you a fixed price. The scope should list exactly what's included, what's not included, and what counts as a scope change. If the agency can't define scope clearly, they haven't understood your requirements well enough.

### Modern Technology Stack

Ask what technologies they use by default. If the answer is "WordPress, PHP, or jQuery," that's fine if you need a brochure site. If you need a real application, you want to hear about modern stacks: Next.js, Astro, React, Go, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Tailwind CSS, Docker, cloud deployment.

The specific technology matters less than whether the agency has a deliberate stack choice. Agencies that use whatever the lead developer knows best are a red flag. Agencies that have standardized on a modern, proven stack are investing in their craft.

### Portfolio of Real Applications

Look at their past work - not just screenshots, but actual deployed applications. Better yet, talk to past clients. A legitimate agency will have a portfolio of functional applications, not just marketing websites.

## How to Read a Software Development Proposal

When you receive a proposal, look for these elements:

**Clear deliverables.** The proposal should list exactly what will be built - screens, features, integrations, data models. Vague language like "intuitive dashboard" or "seamless integration" means the scope is undefined.

**Technology choices explained.** The proposal should explain why each major technology was chosen. If it just says "React frontend with Node.js backend" without any rationale, the agency hasn't thought deeply about your use case.

**Timeline with milestones.** A 6-week project should break down into week-by-week milestones with specific deliverables. If the timeline is a single date, the agency hasn't planned the work.

**Maintenance and support terms.** What happens after launch? How are bug fixes handled? What about feature additions? The proposal should address post-launch support explicitly.

**Pricing breakdown.** You should see how much of the budget goes to development vs project management vs other overhead. Hidden costs are a bad sign.

## Five Questions to Ask Before Hiring

1. **Who will write the code, and how many years of experience do they have?** You want to know the actual developers, not just the company profile.

2. **Can you show me a similar project you've built?** Look for functional similarity (e.g., "you built a logistics portal before"), not just industry match.

3. **What happens if the project needs to change scope mid-way?** A good agency has a formal change request process. A bad one will either refuse changes or add vague "extras" costs.

4. **Who owns the source code and intellectual property?** The answer should always be "you do." If there's any ambiguity, walk away.

5. **What does post-launch support look like?** Get specific: response times, fix SLAs, pricing for ongoing maintenance, and how emergency issues are handled.

## Your Agency Evaluation Checklist

Before you sign anything, check these boxes:

- [ ] You've spoken directly with the senior engineer who will lead the project
- [ ] The agency provided a fixed-price quote with clear scope boundaries
- [ ] They use a modern, deliberate technology stack (not whatever is trendy)
- [ ] They have a portfolio of real, functional applications - not just marketing sites
- [ ] You've talked to at least one past client
- [ ] Source code ownership is explicitly stated in the contract
- [ ] Post-launch support terms are defined
- [ ] The proposal includes a week-by-week or sprint-by-sprint timeline
- [ ] You understand what triggers a scope change and how it's priced
