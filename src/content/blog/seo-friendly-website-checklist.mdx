"SEO-friendly website" is the most-used and least-defined phrase in the Indian web design market. It appears in almost every Pune quote and means almost nothing.

Here is what it actually means, written as a checklist you can verify yourself.

## The Honest Definition

An SEO-friendly website is one that:

1. Can be crawled and indexed completely
2. Loads fast enough to pass Core Web Vitals on mobile
3. Answers a specific question better than the ten results above it
4. Makes its facts unambiguous with structured data
5. Is genuinely useful to a real person on a real phone

That is the whole list. Everything else is tactics that serve one of those five goals.

## Checklist 1: Crawlability

**Is there a robots.txt, and does it block anything you need indexed?**
Check `yourdomain.com/robots.txt`. Confirm your pages, images, and CSS are not disallowed.

**Is there an XML sitemap, and is it in Search Console?**
Google has been explicit: a sitemap helps them understand site structure, priority, and freshness. Most Pune sites either have none or have one nobody submitted.

**Do all your important pages have indexable, canonical URLs?**
Look at any URL in the address bar. If it has tracking parameters, session IDs, or duplicates, you are splitting authority.

**Is anything important hidden behind a click or a login?**
Content behind interaction is not reliably crawled. Google renders JavaScript but does not click buttons for you.

**Do you have a 404 page that returns a real 404 status?**
A soft 404 - a real page returning a 200 status for missing content - tells search engines the page exists. This is a common unnoticed error.

## Checklist 2: Core Web Vitals

This is the one measurable, non-subjective item, and most competitors have failed it.

The 2026 thresholds, at the 75th percentile of real user visits:

| Metric | Good threshold | Field pass rate (mobile) |
|---|---|---|
| LCP - Largest Contentful Paint | Under 2.5s | 63% |
| INP - Interaction to Next Paint | Under 200ms | 63% |
| CLS - Cumulative Layout Shift | Under 0.1 | 77% |

Across 37,036 websites, **52% of page-one Google results fail Core Web Vitals**. Even position one fails 49.2% of the time.

**What this means practically:** speed is the most available SEO advantage in the Pune market. Roughly half the sites outranking anyone have ignored it.

Practical checks:

- **What is your LCP element?** PageSpeed Insights names it. Usually a hero image or logo lockup.
- **Is your hero image sized for its actual display dimensions?** Serving a 3000px image to a 400px-wide phone slot is the single most common failure.
- **Do you lazy-load below-the-fold images?** The median page weighs 2.6MB across 71 requests.
- **How much JavaScript runs before interaction?** If INP is failing, this is why.
- **Does your host respond in under 800ms?** Time to First Byte matters, and cheap shared hosting is the most common cause of LCP failure.

Framework field data, because this is measurable and not a matter of opinion:

| Framework | Mobile CWV pass rate |
|---|---|
| Astro | 48% |
| WordPress | 38.4% |
| Next.js | 29.3% |

## Checklist 3: Structured Data

Structured data does not rank you directly. It makes your entity unambiguous, which improves the chance of accurate citation in AI answers and enables rich results.

The 2026 baseline for a Pune business:

| Page type | Schema to add |
|---|---|
| Homepage | `Organization`, `WebSite`, `LocalBusiness` with accurate NAP and geo |
| Service pages | `Service` referencing the organization by `@id` |
| Every page except home | `BreadcrumbList` |
| Blog posts | `Article` with author, `datePublished`, `dateModified` |
| FAQ sections | `FAQPage` |
| Product pages | `Product` with price and availability |

**Two 2026 changes worth knowing.** Google's May 2026 AI Search guide states there is no special schema for AI search - structured data remains useful for rich results eligibility but is not privileged for generative AI. And FAQ rich results stopped displaying in Google Search on 7 May 2026, though the markup still helps answer engines parse question-and-answer pairs.

**Validate before deploying.** Malformed markup gets ignored wholesale. Our [schema playbook](/blog/schema-markup-structured-data-playbook) has working JSON-LD examples including the `@id` entity-graph technique.

## Checklist 4: Content That Answers Something Specific

This is where most Pune websites fail completely, and no amount of technical work compensates for it.

**The test:** does each page have a question it answers better than the ten results above it?

Analysis of 1,000 pages frequently cited by AI assistants found consistent patterns: short paragraphs averaging about three sentences, heavy list usage, and explicit question-and-answer formatting. Those structures are also just good writing.

Specifically:

- **Open each section with the conclusion,** then support it. If someone has to reconstruct your answer, it will not be quoted or retained.
- **Use specific numbers with attribution.** "Most businesses" is vague. "52% of page-one results fail Core Web Vitals" is extractable and citable.
- **Build topic clusters.** Your service page, FAQ, case study, and supporting articles should link to each other. An engine trying to establish what you do reads a cluster, not an orphan page.
- **State your boundaries clearly.** Name your services, your location, your audience, and what you do not do. Machine systems need unambiguous entity definitions.
- **Original data beats synthesis.** Your own delivery times, costs, and before-and-after metrics are the hardest thing for a competitor to replicate and the easiest for an engine to cite.

**The anti-pattern to avoid:** generic AI-generated service copy. It reads as filler because it is filler, and it actively dilutes a page that would otherwise rank.

## Checklist 5: Mobile Usability and Accessibility

India sits at roughly 80% mobile traffic share, so this is effectively the whole audit.

- **No horizontal scroll** at 390px width
- **Tap targets at least 24x24 CSS pixels** per WCAG 2.2 Level AA
- **Focus visible** when tabbing, and not hidden by sticky headers - WCAG 2.2 added Focus Not Obscured (Minimum) at AA
- **Text contrast at least 4.5:1** for normal text
- **Alt text** on meaningful images
- **A single-pointer alternative** to any drag interaction - also new in WCAG 2.2
- **No hover-only actions** - there is no hover on a touchscreen
- **Plain language** - which helps non-native English speakers, busy readers, and ranking simultaneously

Accessibility and SEO are the same optimisation here. Semantic HTML helps crawlers; plain language matches natural search intent.

## The 20-Point Scorecard

Print this and hand it to whoever built your site.

**Technical (8 points)**
1. Robots.txt present and not blocking important pages
2. XML sitemap submitted to Search Console
3. Canonical URLs on every page
4. No soft 404s
5. LCP under 2.5s on mobile
6. INP under 200ms
7. CLS under 0.1
8. Page weight under 1.5MB

**Structured data (4 points)**
9. Organization schema sitewide
10. LocalBusiness schema with accurate NAP, hours, and geo
11. Service schema on each service page
12. Validated with Google's tools - no errors

**Content (5 points)**
13. Every service has its own page
14. Each page opens with a direct answer
15. Specific numbers rather than vague claims
16. FAQ blocks with real customer questions
17. Internal linking forms topic clusters

**Local (3 points)**
18. NAP identical across website, Google Business Profile, and directories
19. Google Business Profile has photos, services, and recent posts
20. Real reviews on Google plus one vertical directory presence

**18+ means genuinely SEO-friendly. Below 14 means there is real work available.**

## What to Do First

**Week 1 - Facts.** Verify NAP consistency everywhere. Confirm Google Business Profile is complete with photos and recent posts. This is unglamorous and it is the foundation for both AI surfaces.

**Week 2 - Speed.** Fix the LCP element on your three highest-traffic pages. Compress hero images, lazy-load the rest, check your TTFB.

**Week 3 - Structure.** Add Organization, LocalBusiness, Service, and BreadcrumbList. Validate everything.

**Week 4 - Content.** Take your three most commercially important pages. Open each section with a direct answer. Break long paragraphs to roughly three sentences. Add a real FAQ. Convert one vague claim into a specific number.

**Ongoing.** Run your real customer questions through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Mode monthly. Record what they say and which sources they cite. That is the only reliable measurement of AI visibility.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most important factor in SEO-friendly web design?**
There is no single factor. For a Pune business in 2026, the practical order is: crawlability, then Core Web Vitals, then content that answers something specific. Technical work gets you indexed; content gets you chosen.

**Do I need an SEO-friendly website if I run ads?**
If you run ads, you need conversion and speed, not SEO. But your Quality Score is affected by landing page experience, which overlaps with Core Web Vitals. And you are paying rent on traffic you could earn free.

**Is a website built with a website builder SEO-friendly?**
It can be. Wix and Squarespace produce indexable, reasonably fast sites. The gap versus custom is performance ceiling and structural control, not a fundamental SEO disadvantage. For a 5-page business site the gap rarely justifies the cost.

**How long does it take to make a website SEO-friendly?**
The technical items - speed, schema, crawlability - take one to two weeks. Meaningful content restructuring takes one to two months. Results appear over three to six months. Anyone promising page one in a month is selling something else.

**Does page speed really affect rankings?**
The direct correlation is weak - around 0.09 for LCP, meaning speed explains under 1% of ranking variation. The indirect effects are larger: click-through rate, conversion rate, and crawl budget. Passing is not required to rank, but 52% of page-one results currently fail.

**Should I hire an SEO agency separately?**
If your site is technically sound and content is good, ongoing SEO work - content, links, local citations - is worth outsourcing. If the site has never been audited, fix technical fundamentals first. Agencies commonly sell link packages to sites that cannot rank for technical reasons.
