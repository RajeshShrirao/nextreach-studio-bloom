## Website Builders: Genuinely Enough for Many Businesses

Wix and Squarespace have improved to the point where criticism of them is often out of date. For a business that needs to exist online and look credible, they are a legitimate choice.

**Where they work well:**

- Service businesses that need pages, a contact form, and a WhatsApp button
- Anyone who must update content themselves without a developer
- Projects where ₹10,000 is the real budget
- Getting something live this week

**Where they stop working:**

- Anything requiring login, a database, or user-specific content
- Deep integration with your CRM, ERP, or payment system
- Performance requirements where you need control over JavaScript weight
- Migration later. Wix and Squarespace exports are functional but not clean.

**Worth noting:** Wix powers roughly 4.3% of all websites and holds about 45% of the DIY builder market. Squarespace was acquired for $7.2 billion and continues to invest in AI generation. These are serious products, not toys.

**The honest Pune read:** if your requirement is "a 5-page site with a contact form and I need to edit the text myself," a builder is a defensible ₹10,000 decision. Do not pay an agency ₹60,000 to do that with a builder.

## WordPress: Maximum Flexibility With A Real Cost

WordPress powers a large share of the web. That flexibility is genuine - and the trade-offs are real too.

**The advantage:** you own the code. Theme, plugins, hosting, database - all yours. There is a developer or plugin for almost any requirement. It is the standard choice for content-heavy sites.

**The cost, honestly stated:**

- **Security surface.** WordPress is the most targeted CMS in existence. Updates are not optional. SiteGround, WP Engine, or a competent developer required.
- **Performance.** WordPress passes Core Web Vitals on only 38.4% of sites - the lowest rate of any major CMS measured across 3.7M websites. Plugin count is the primary cause.
- **Total cost of ownership.** This is where buyers get surprised. A ₹40,000 build becomes ₹1,30,000 over three years once hosting, premium plugins, and maintenance are counted.
- **The plugin question.** Ask any WordPress site owner how many plugins they have. Ask which do anything. The honest answer is usually they do not know.

**WordPress is the right choice when** you run a content-heavy site, a non-technical person must publish, you need specific plugins, and you have someone responsible for maintenance.

## Hosted Platforms: Shopify and Webflow

Different tools for different jobs, bundled as "hosted platforms."

**Shopify** - commerce. See our [e-commerce platform guide](/blog/ecommerce-website-design-pune) for the full comparison.

**Webflow** - marketing sites with serious design flexibility and CMS capability, no plugin security burden, and excellent animation. Strong choice for design-led marketing sites. Weaker than custom on performance ceilings, and it is proprietary.

**The honest limitation:** both are platforms you rent capability from. Shopify costs roughly ₹2,000/month before transaction fees. Webflow charges by site tier. Neither is a bad deal - but the rent is permanent.

## Custom Development: What Actually Justifies It

**Justified when:**

- You need authentication, roles, a database, or user-specific state
- Your workflow has logic no plugin or platform models - unusual pricing, approval chains, custom manufacturing processes
- You are integrating systems that do not have clean connectors
- Performance is directly tied to revenue and you have exhausted platform options
- You will own and extend the system for years

**Not justified when:**

- You want a 6-page marketing site
- You need a content site someone else can publish to
- A ₹60,000 WordPress build would meet the requirement
- You are choosing custom because a competitor chose custom

**The honest cost framing.** Custom development is not expensive per feature - it is expensive per ongoing change. You are buying the ability to modify anything, forever. That is genuinely valuable when your business logic is unusual and worthless when it is not.

## The Three-Year Cost Comparison

This is the comparison most quotes omit. Assume a marketing site, 6 pages, no custom functionality.

| | Year 1 | Year 3 total | You own the code? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Builder** | ₹10,000 build + ₹8,000/yr | ₹34,000 | No |
| **WordPress** | ₹40,000 build + ₹30,000/yr | ₹130,000 | Yes |
| **Webflow** | ₹50,000 build + ₹24,000/yr | ₹122,000 | Partial |
| **Custom (Astro)** | ₹80,000 build + ₹15,000/yr | ₹125,000 | Yes |
| **Custom (WordPress agency)** | ₹40,000 build + ₹35,000/yr | ₹145,000 | Yes |

**Two conclusions.**

First, **total ownership cost converges.** WordPress, Webflow, and a lean custom build land within ₹20,000 of each other over three years. The differentiator is not cost - it is capability and lock-in.

Second, **a well-built custom site can be cheaper than WordPress over three years.** This surprises people, but it follows directly from WordPress's maintenance burden. ₹40,000 upfront plus ₹35,000/year of security patching and plugin work exceeds a ₹80,000 static build plus ₹15,000/year.

This is why we build on Astro and Next.js rather than WordPress. Field data shows Astro-based sites pass Core Web Vitals on 48% of sites against WordPress at 38.4% - the architecture difference is measurable in production.

## The Lock-In Question

Ask two questions:

**1. If I want to leave in two years, what happens?**
- Builder: export your content and start over. Painful but survivable.
- WordPress: export everything, move hosting, change theme. Manageable.
- Shopify: export products, rebuild the theme. Harder than expected.
- Custom: you have a repository. Walk away.

**2. If my developer disappears tomorrow, who maintains this?**
- Builder: anyone with a login.
- WordPress: a competent freelancer, ₹20,000-₹40,000/month.
- Shopify: Shopify experts, or an agency.
- Custom: only someone who understands the codebase - and codebases age.

That second question is the one that matters most. A custom site with no documentation and no accessible engineer is a liability, not an asset.

## How to Decide in Five Minutes

Answer honestly:

1. **Do I need users to log in or store data?** Yes → Custom or WordPress with specific plugins. No → Consider a builder.
2. **Must I edit content myself regularly?** Yes → WordPress, Webflow, or a builder. No → Custom is viable.
3. **Does my workflow have logic no platform models?** Yes → Custom. No → Platform.
4. **Is performance directly tied to revenue?** Yes → Custom, measured. No → Platform is fine.
5. **Can I afford ₹15,000-₹40,000/year for maintenance?** No → Builder, honestly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is WordPress outdated in 2026?**
No. It remains the dominant CMS and is the right answer for content-heavy sites with non-technical publishers. What is outdated is WordPress used for things it is bad at - performance-critical sites and lightweight marketing pages.

**Is WordPress better than custom development?**
For a content site where someone must publish without a developer, yes. For a performance-critical or application-like build, no. It is a tool-fit question, not a quality hierarchy.

**Should a Pune business use Wix instead of hiring an agency?**
If your requirement is a 5-page site with a contact form, yes - and you should save the agency fee. If you need CRM integration, login, or custom functionality, an agency is the right call.

**How much does a custom website cost in Pune?**
₹1,50,000 and up for anything with real functionality. A marketing site on a modern framework is ₹30,000-₹80,000. Our [full cost breakdown](/blog/website-design-cost-pune-2026) has all the tiers.

**Can I migrate WordPress to a custom build later?**
Yes, and content migration is the straightforward part. The harder problem is redirects - every URL needs mapping or you lose rankings. See our [redesign guide](/blog/website-redesign-pune).

**Is Webflow better than WordPress?**
For design-led marketing sites with animation and CMS needs, often yes, and it has no plugin security burden. For deep plugin ecosystems and B2B features, WordPress. Webflow is proprietary and costs more at scale.

**Which is best for SEO?**
No platform ranks better on merit. What ranks is content quality, structure, speed, and structured data. Astro-based sites pass Core Web Vitals most often in the framework data, which gives a measurable edge.
